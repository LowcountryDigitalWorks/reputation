## Checklist — each new client/location

1. **Qualification:** Determine whether existing CRM/POS/booking/GBP native features already solve the need. Do not ingest a new customer list or install a parallel review platform without a measured gap. Confirm client owner and acceptable service cost.
2. **Identity/tenancy:** Owner KEEP and 3-code agency entitlement are recorded in #652, with LDW and separate test projects identified; verify each **new real client** project's isolation/permissions and account-level access in its exact approval scope, not from one LDW project read. Maintain tenant-scoped identities, separate projects, explicit staff roles, export/offboarding map.
3. **Destinations:** Administrator configures official business-controlled Google/Facebook link. Validate HTTPS, legitimate location, and closed redirect destinations. A review QR must **not** encode names, emails, transaction IDs, visit dates, sentiment, score, or other per-recipient data. For the simplest direct Google link/QR, a verified business owner/authorized operator can use Google Business Profile's desktop-browser **Read reviews → Get more reviews**, then **Copy** the official link or right-click and save its QR image; Google's own [current instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815) say QR generation is desktop-only. Prefer this native customer-owned feature before any custom QR generator or hosted intermediary. It does **not** waive client approval for the actual final QR/link placement or a real-device scan of the final medium; do not replace the separately proven DFD private QR or distribute it without clinic authorization.
4. **Neutrality:** The *same* honest-public-review opportunity is always available for every eligible recipient. Test both one-star and five-star branches and negative/private feedback paths. If any public link appears only for positives, **STOP** (Google selective solicitation rule). No rewards or star targets; no request to mention staff or revise/remove reviews. Google expressly prohibits **selectively soliciting positive reviews** and **discouraging negative reviews**, even when the review request appears to be a generic feedback workflow. [Google Maps policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114).
5. **Communications:** Default no new send. One prior owner-controlled synthetic email request with all five rating choices and unsubscribe was already delivered; do **not** repeat it simply to generate proof. For a separately authorized live client, validate exact template, opt-out, reminder/cadence, dedupe and suppression. SMS requires customer consent, local law/A2P review, provider setup and variable-cost approval.
6. **Responses:** Review monitoring can produce drafts. Human approval before *every* public owner reply. MGR automatic Google/Facebook replies OFF during initial use. Never use vendor AI review-writing prompts as a way to pressure or manufacture customer reviews.
7. **Widget:** Only verified genuine authorized reviews, with rights to display, current source/attribution and no synthetic provider samples. Restrict widget allowed domains; website ORCH accepts actual CSP, performance and mobile layout before publish.
8. **Evidence:** Track eligibility/request/delivery/click separately from externally observed review/reply, with source, timestamp, location, actor, test/prod class and permitted retention. Never infer that one click caused a particular Google review.
9. **Security:** Keep credentials in approved stores; use short-lived/revocable project-scoped connection where available. Treat API/webhook events as untrusted until authenticating signatures, proving retry/idempotency and tenant mapping. Never put PHI in MGR; provider is not positioned as offering a healthcare BAA.
10. **Offboarding:** Export customers and reviews through documented CSV where authorized; separately inventory review pages, widgets, tags, sources, locations, templates, automations, domains and provider integrations because whole-project export remains unproven. Verify customer review destinations remain owned outside the provider; revoke MGR keys/connections and plan any widget swap.
11. **Commercial acceptance:** Record operator minutes per setup/month, additional location/client margin, request volume, support incidents, variable email/SMS cost, quality of reporting, agency entitlement, exact refund/renewal dates and rollback plan before any customer offer.

## Federal review-law and Google-platform policy — separate checks (FTC verified November 14, 2026)

**Scope and authority:** LDW offers reputation services and may be responsible for its own representations, requests, moderation and third-party agency conduct. The FTC's [Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, 16 CFR Part 465](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) has applied since **October 21, 2024**; [FTC guidance for marketers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/soliciting-paying-online-reviews-guide-marketers) and Google's independent [Maps user-contributed content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) also matter. **These are distinct layers; do not assert that every violation of a platform policy is necessarily a violation of Part 465.** This operator summary is not a legal determination or substitute for qualified review of proposed client campaigns.

| Control | FTC rule / FTC Act distinction | Google review destination — LDW default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authenticity / source | Part 465 forbids specified fake or false review and testimonial conduct, including procuring or disseminating covered fake material under the rule's applicable knowledge standards; ordinary neutral requests to genuine customers are not categorically prohibited. | Require a genuine customer experience; never create, write, procure, seed or pose as a reviewer. |
| Compensation / incentives | **Part 465.4** bans explicitly or implicitly conditioning review incentives on a **particular sentiment**. FTC guidance says neutral incentives are not automatically banned by that section, but disclosure/FTC Act obligations may still apply. | **No incentives for posting, changing or removing any Google review**, positive or negative. A neutral incentive can still violate Google's stricter rule. |
| Insider or connected reviews | **Part 465.5** addresses specified insider reviews/endorsements without clear material-relationship disclosure; additional FTC Act duties can apply. | Do not solicit owners, employees, family, contractors or other conflicted parties to manipulate Google ratings; never presume a disclosure overrides a platform ban. |
| Review suppression / solicitation bias | **Part 465.7** specifically addresses review suppression through defined threats/intimidation, unfounded legal threats and specified misrepresentations. FTC [Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) notes that **selectively asking predicted-happy customers is not itself expressly banned by Part 465**, but may violate broader **Section 5 of the FTC Act**. | Google **expressly bars discouraging negative reviews and selectively soliciting positive reviews**. A positive-only public link or obscure negative-only reveal path returns `RATING_ROUTE_POLICY_HOLD`, regardless of whether Part 465.7 alone covers it. |
| Testimonials / display | Republishing a review as promotional testimonial is different from merely hosting reviews; authenticity, representative presentation, permissions and disclosure require separate assessment. | No fabricated testimonials, invented star counts, selective misleading widgets or claims that MGR feedback is a published Google review. |
| Business-controlled independence | Part 465.6 bars certain company-controlled review sites falsely presented as independent. | Hosted feedback pages must identify the business/provider accurately, never claim independent review certification without evidence. |

**Operator preflight for any authorized campaign:** identify the real client and channel; confirm **no reward or exchange** for any Google review; review exact request copy, routing and whether all eligible experiences can reach the genuine business-controlled public link; inspect any insider or testimonial disclosures, widget moderation/claims, and abusive removal/revision requests; confirm suppression/opt-out, tenant authorization and privacy rules; require named human approval. Preserve supporting facts privately; publish only non-sensitive pass/fail gate state. If uncertain, mark `LEGAL_PLATFORM_POLICY_REVIEW_HOLD` and use a direct neutral review link only after client authorization. **LDW's owner-witnessed all-five public Google access is accepted in its narrow scope; it does not waive a different client's own exact-page neutral-route, consent, privacy and release checks, G1's healthcare gate, or G3b's no-launch decision.**

## MGR webhook event semantics — provider-documented safety gate (November 2026)

MGR's [first-party Webhooks guide](https://docs.moregoodreviews.com/platform/webhooks) says **`review-created` can fire when an email/SMS recipient clicks a rating link and the landing page saves the selected rating, before any form text or feedback is submitted**. A later **`review-updated`** may indicate that MGR's *first-party feedback form* has received additional content, but can also represent an update to existing MGR feedback. Neither event is an independently published **Google/Facebook review** or an authorized public-reply action. `message-created` can mean **created or scheduled**, not delivered; MGR separates message update from actual delivery state.

**Evidence classification (for any future, separately authorized integration):**

| MGR event | What the event can establish | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `review-created` | An MGR project rating/feedback record was created; may be the **rating-link click alone** | Customer submitted the form, public Google review published, net new public reviews, authentic patient/customer testimonial |
| `review-updated` | MGR's existing rating/feedback record was updated, possibly after written feedback | A new unique external public review or accepted public owner reply |
| `message-created` | MGR request message created or **scheduled** | Provider delivered it, recipient read it or a public review resulted |
| `message-updated` | An MGR message status changed | Receipt/consent, successful delivery, review publication or customer-report acceptance |

**Security and privacy hold:** MGR's guide states webhooks can send the **full review/message payload**, are retried on non-successful endpoint responses, and include `X-Webhook-Signature` as a `sha256=` HMAC-SHA256 digest of the **exact raw body** using the per-project API Secret Key. Such a provider signature only supports origin/integrity verification after correct secret handling; it is **not** tenant/customer authorization or proof of published Google review. An accepted implementation would additionally need exact project/tenant authorization, approved data minimization, secret rotation and secure storage, replay/idempotency controls, retry/reconciliation plan, bounded retention, and customer/consent/regulated-data assessments. Never copy raw webhook bodies, signatures, names, review text, patient/PHI or API keys into public GitHub, ordinary logs or the private research shelf.

**Current release status: `WEBHOOK_NOT_CONFIGURED_BY_REPUTATION / NO_EVENT_INGESTION / NO_CUSTOMER_DELIVERY`.** The project-scoped connector and this playbook do not authorize creating a webhook, enabling the automations, configuring a receiver, capturing review text, changing the tenant, submitting a synthetic rating, producing a customer report, or treating any MGR record as an externally verified public review. The accepted [offline aggregate comparator](https://github.com/LowcountryDigitalWorks/reputation/tree/main/proofs/aggregate-monitor) remains provider-independent and never ingests these webhook events. Google public-review observations must continue to come from a separately authorized, trustworthy native source and human review process.
